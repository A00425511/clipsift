How to label

Work in this order:

  1. Look at the frame.
  2. Play the clip (Space).
  3. Switch to Movement view (M).
  4. Then finish marking.
  • Mark what is in this frame, not what shows up somewhere else in the clip.
  • Movement counts as evidence. An animal that is hard to see in the still but moves in the clip is a confident mark.
  • Mark every animal you are confident about. One click per animal. Pick the type before you click: fish (F) or invertebrate such as a crab (I).
  • Use Unsure (U) for maybes: something that might be an animal but does not meet the rule. Faint fish in a school are the usual case. Unsure marks are never counted.
  • Press No animals (N) only after you have checked both the clip and the Movement view. The button asks for both.
  • Use Not fish (X) sparingly: at most 3 per frame, and only for things that truly look like a fish. Skip the pipe and ordinary seabed, because software handles those.
  • You are confident when you can see at least two of these: shape, anatomy, colour or a flash, movement.
  • Right-click a dot to change its type (including to or from Unsure), set a species if you know it, or remove it. Species is optional. The list shows fish for a fish mark and invertebrates for an invertebrate mark, and you can switch between common and scientific names.
  • Stick to your own name. Do not discuss frames until both people have saved them.

Zoom with the mouse wheel and drag to move. Hold Space (or press Motion) to play the 10 second clip in the picture, 5 seconds before to 5 seconds after the frame. It is the same zoom, and your dots stay on top. It starts from the beginning each time; turn off Restart each time (R) to carry on from where it stopped. Drag the white playhead to move through the clip, and use Count second only (O) to loop just the count second. Use 0.25x to slow it down. Count only what is in the frame during the Count frame second. Dots cannot be added while the clip plays. Enhance (C) has Light, Strong and Silhouette settings; they change the display only. Movement view shows what changed, in black and white, and you can mark in either view. Click a dot to remove it. Another person labels every frame too, on their own. You never see their marks, and there are no computer suggestions here, so label what you see.
