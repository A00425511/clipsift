              What this tool does

              ClipSift walks you through Coastal Action's video analysis protocol. It sets the analysis window, cuts every count point out of the video, works out the results and writes the report. You judge visibility, name the species and decide what counts.

              The five steps for each video

              1. Visibility: 2 to 3 people each choose a category.
              2. Watch window: watch the whole window in real time and list every species.
              3. Count points: for each point, watch the clip, mark what you are sure of, check the numbers and save. Count only in the highlighted second. Use 0.25x to slow it down.
              4. Quality check: a second person counts the selected points without seeing the first counts.
              5. Report: print it or save it as a PDF.

              Adding a new video and getting a report

              Press New video, choose a video, then press Start. You can keep working while it runs, and add more videos to the list. When it is ready, open it and count the points. Press Report any time for the counts, times and species so far. Print it, or choose Save as PDF in the print window.

              What the timeline markers mean

              • Grey tick: a point to count. Click it to open it.
              • Green tick: you have counted this point.
              • Shaded band: the analysis window, the part of the video that is used.

              The counting rules, in brief

              • Count only what is inside the one designated second, which starts at the count point. A fish that appears within about half a second either side at 0.25x may count. Past that, add a note instead.
              • Another fish may count only if you are completely sure it is a different fish. The rule exists to avoid counting one fish twice.
              • Count an animal only if two of these four tell it apart: shape, anatomical features, colour or flashes, swimming or movement.
              • When unsure, leave it out. Counting too few is better than counting too many.
              • Movement alone is not counted. In a school, count only the fish you can confirm, and tick the note for the rest.
              • Counts are a relative index, not how many animals are really there.

              What suggested counts are

              Model suggestions are off each time you open a recording, so they do not sway your count. Tick Show model suggestions under Mark what you see to see the boxes and the numbers. They are rough: the model flags seafloor and the pipe, and is not trained on this water. The tool may show a count made by software. It is only a hint to compare with, and it is never filled in for you. Your count is the one that is saved.
