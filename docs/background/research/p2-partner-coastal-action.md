### Climate-driven range shifts
- Gulf of Maine and Scotian Shelf had a regime shift around 2010; many fish and invertebrates have moved north, some deeper. [snippet] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8445949
- DFO added blackbelly rosefish and john dory to its summer Scotian Shelf/Bay of Fundy survey in 2020; abundance rising but still rare. [snippet] CBC link above.
- Dalhousie's Boris Worm links diver-photographed tropical strays to Gulf Stream eddies and warming; he says the fish themselves are unlikely to disrupt ecosystems. [snippet] https://ckpgtoday.ca/?p=249356 (news; 2019-2021, older than 3 years).
- Bottom water off NS warmed about 3C vs about 1.5C at surface over 30 years (EU Copernicus report, not DFO); DFO saw slight Scotian Shelf cooling in 2023. [snippet] https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/climate/impacts/ocean-floor-off-n-s-warming-twice-as-much-as-surface-report-finds
- Models: cold-water species (cod, haddock, redfish, plaice) lose habitat; spiny dogfish and lobster gain. [snippet] PMC8445949.

### Regulation and why video is favoured
- Capturing fish for research needs a DFO scientific licence under the Fisheries Act (free, up to 45 days to process, Pacific page); applications must describe mortality precautions and non-target handling; catch reports required. [snippet] https://www.pac.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/fm-gp/licence-permis/scientific-scientifique-eng.html
- SARA s. 32 bans killing, harming, capturing or taking listed Endangered/Threatened species; research that may affect them needs a SARA permit. [snippet] https://dfo-mpo.gc.ca/species-especes/license-permis-eng.html
- Animal care (CCAC) and any DFO statement favouring video: not found. [unconfirmed]. "Video avoids permits and handling stress" is an [inference] consistent with the above, not a quoted policy.

### Analyst workflow
- BRUVS standard: fixed 60-min annotated window, at least 2 trained annotators, record MaxN per species (max count in a single frame). [snippet] https://globalfinprint.org/about/_assets/global-finprint-basic-bruvs-protocol.pdf
- Time: Australian AIMS figure of about 4,000 video-hours per year needing about 12,000 analyst-hours, so about 3 analyst-hours per video-hour. [snippet via Frontiers] https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.944582/pdf . A US SOP reviews 2-hour clips at up to 3x speed. [snippet] https://ftp.sccwrp.org/pub/download/PROJECTS/EMPA/sopPDFs/SOP092623/manuals/SOP8_BRUVs.pdf
- Staff cost: no published dollar figure found. Multiply local wage by about 3. [inference]
- Machine-assisted annotation: F1 0.79 on 12 species, only about 12% of fish got a species label. [snippet] Frontiers link above.

## What this means for our POC
1. Scope narrowly: a human-in-the-loop triage tool (flag frames with fish, propose a species with confidence, log MaxN), not a classifier that replaces the analyst. Fits the fatigue and cost pain points.
2. Data is the risk. No NS-specific labelled turbid footage found, so ask for sample clips first; fall back to public datasets (e.g. Fish4Knowledge or OzFish-style sets, not verified) and state the domain gap honestly.
3. Make the species list editable and versioned (add/remove, "unknown / needs expert"), with at-risk and invasive flags. This directly answers the "outdated species list" issue and is cheap to build.
4. Demo the turbidity problem: show a before/after with contrast enhancement and detection confidence dropping with turbidity, rather than claiming it is solved.
5. Frame the business case with the 3 analyst-hours per video-hour benchmark, labelled as Australian and to be replaced with Coastal Action's own number; stay on regulatory logic (fewer licences, no handling) as the "why".

## 10 questions to ask the partner or mentors
1. Can you share sample footage (even 10-20 clips), ideally including murky and clear days, with permission terms for hackathon use?
2. What camera(s) do you use (make, resolution, frame rate, lighting, stereo or single, baited or unbaited, fixed or towed)?
3. Typical clip length and how many hours per season?
4. Do you have an existing species list (and a labelled dataset, even a spreadsheet of past IDs)?
5. Which sites and habitats (eelgrass, tidal creeks, fishways, LaHave, Mahone Bay living shoreline) matter most for the demo?
6. Is the target metric presence/absence, MaxN, or full counts and sizes?
7. Who analyses video today, how many hours per video-hour, and what does it cost you?
8. What error rate or confidence do you need before a result is acceptable, especially for species at risk and invasives?
9. Where would the tool run (laptop, cloud, no internet) and who are the end users' skill levels? Any data-sharing limits (e.g. sensitive at-risk locations)?
10. Do you work with DFO, Dalhousie or Innovasea on video already, and is any of that code or data open to us?
