            What this tool does

            ClipSift helps you count fish and other animals in underwater video, one short moment at a time. It picks the moments and cuts the clips; you decide what is really there.

            Your first count in 5 steps

            1. Type your name in the box at the top. It is saved with your counts.
            2. Pick a point on the timeline, or use the first one in the list on the left.
            3. Watch the clip. Count only in the green COUNT NOW second. Use 0.25x to slow it down.
            4. Click each animal on the still picture to mark it, then check the numbers in Your count.
            5. Press Save and go to the next. Repeat until the list says everything is counted.

            What the timeline markers mean

            • Grey tick: a point to count. Click it to open it.
            • Green tick: you have counted this point.
            • Shaded band: the analysis window, the part of the video that is used.
            • Blue block (More tools): motion found here. A supporting check.
            • Dark block (More tools): set aside, no activity found. The time is kept.
            • Dashed block marked A (More tools): a random check of set-aside time.
            • Hatched block marked ! (More tools): camera shake or low visibility.
            • Green underline and a tick (More tools): reviewed.

            The counting rules, in brief

            • Count only what is inside the one designated second. A fish that appears within half a second either side may count.
            • Count an animal only if two of these four tell it apart: shape, anatomical features, colour or flashes, swimming or movement.
            • When unsure, leave it out. Counting too few is better than counting too many.
            • Movement alone is not counted. Tick the movement note instead.
            • Counts are a relative index, not how many animals are really there.

            What suggested counts are

            Model suggestions are off each time you open a recording, so they do not sway your count. Tick Show model suggestions under Mark what you see to see the boxes and the numbers. They are rough: the model flags seafloor and the pipe, and is not trained on this water. The tool may show a count made by software. It is only a hint to compare with, and it is never filled in for you. In this prototype the software is a simple stand-in that reacts to any movement, so expect it to be wrong. Your count is the one that is saved.
